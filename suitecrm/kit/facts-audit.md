# How this fact sheet was built, and what was thrown away

The sheet beside this file is short on purpose. It was produced in three passes: one pass read
the repository and wrote down everything it believed; a second pass attacked every claim; a third
kept only what it could re-verify itself.

**32 facts kept, 10 dropped.** That ratio is the useful part of this page: facts about a
repository that cannot go stale are harder to produce than they look, and a sheet that is 40%
wrong is worse than no sheet, because an agent follows it.

## The cases beside this sheet, and the three passes they survived

The questions in `cases/` were drawn from the same reading of the repository, and then put
through three independent passes, each of which found what the one before it had let through.

**One** checked that every keyed path exists and belongs. It corrected keys and rejected nothing,
which is why there is a second: a check that refuses nothing cannot tell a good question from a
lenient reviewer.

**Two** was forced to state, for every question, the one sentence that says what makes a reply
right — and to name its three worst. It found questions whose "which single X" had eight valid
answers, keys that credited one of thirty-eight files carrying the same generated banner, and a
key that named a file as evidence of *absence*, which a correct reply cannot cite.

**Three** repaired the keys pass two judged incomplete, under one standing rule: a key is never
fitted to what an agent would find. Where a question was too broad to have a bounded answer, it
was narrowed rather than padded — and in one case it refuted pass two's complaint instead of
obeying it.

**10 of 10 candidates ship.** What was dropped, and why:

- candidate 15 — near-duplicate of candidate 7
- candidate 8 — near-duplicate of candidate 10
- candidate 6 — The only candidate for which I cannot state an answer criterion. Two of its four clauses are not single-valued and the key resolves both by fiat: 'the three field-type directories that ship templates but no class of their own' is keyed as one arbitrarily chosen .tpl per directory (Link/DetailView.tpl, URL/EditView.tpl, Iframe/DetailView.tpl), so naming the directories the question actually asks for scores zero on 3 of 8 paths; and 'two call sites from outside the field framework' has thirteen valid answers in the tree, of which the key keeps two. The underlying repository fact is genuinely good — exactly three class-less directories, verified across all 35 — which is what makes the encoding so wasteful.
- candidate 9 — It punishes the better answer twice. 'For one scheme' admits five identical directories (Dawn, Day, Dusk, Night, Noon) and the key accepts only Dawn, so an equally correct walk through Day forfeits half the paths. And the key calls four files 'the real inputs' when themes/SuiteP/css/Dawn/style.scss @imports about fifty more partials from ../bootstrap/ and ../suitep-base/, so the agent that actually traces the compile graph scores no better than the one that stops at the directory listing. An arbitrary instantiation plus an incomplete key is the worst combination available.
- candidate 12 — Three of five clauses are arbitrated rather than answered, and the key misses the mechanism's own data files. include/SugarObjects/templates/ holds six templates and the key names two, as bean base classes (Person.php, Company.php), while VardefManager.php:118 actually merges templates/<t>/vardefs.php — absent from the key entirely. include/SugarObjects/implements/ holds three directories and the key names one. 'Which two module classes are the clearest examples' is explicitly a judgment call, with Lead, Prospect, Employee and User equally qualified, so no reply can be recognised as right or wrong on that clause.

### How completeness is scored

A key file whose basename appears in the question or anywhere in the sheet is struck off before
scoring, so the sheet can never be credited for handing over its own answer. That subset is
identical for both arms and is what the completeness figure in `RESULTS.md` is computed on.
